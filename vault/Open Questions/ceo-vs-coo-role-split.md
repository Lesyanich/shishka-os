# When does Bas take operational chores off Lesia so she can stay strategic?

> [!question] Open
> At what trigger does the CEO/COO role split formalise so that WiFi, POS, equipment runs, and supplier visits stop landing on Lesia's plate?

## Context

Today Lesia carries both strategic (menu, finance direction, partner relations) and operational (WiFi outages, POS issues, equipment runs) load. Bas is co-owner but the role split isn't formalised. The COO agent already has a guard rail — `feedback_coo_no_ceo_chores.md` says these tasks must not appear in CEO reports because they distract — but that only treats the symptom, not the structure.

## Why It Matters

L2 opening will roughly double operational surface area (second site, second supplier network, second staff roster). Without a clean split before then, Lesia's strategic time gets eaten by tactical fires and the menu/finance agenda stalls. A formal split also lets agents route tasks correctly instead of guessing who owns what.

## Options Under Consideration

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| A — Bas owns operations end-to-end, Lesia stays strategic | Clean separation; agents route by role; matches CEO/COO archetype | Requires Bas to take on visible accountability; needs a handover plan and check-in cadence |
| B — Lesia keeps full ownership, automate ops via agents | No human re-org needed; leverages existing agent stack | Agents can't physically swap a router or meet a supplier; ceiling on what automation removes |
| C — Split by domain (Bas: Locations + Procurement, Lesia: Menu + Finance) | Plays to each owner's strengths; clear MC routing rules | Cross-domain tasks (e.g. equipment for a new dish) need a tiebreaker; risks duplicate effort |

## Decision Pending On

- [[People/Lesia]] and [[People/Bas]] — joint owners of the call
- L2 opening — operational load spike will force the structure either way; better to decide before than after
