# Kitchen Philosophy

Distilled from owner interview (2026-04-07). Full chef-grade detail in [`docs/bible/kitchen-philosophy.md`](../../docs/bible/kitchen-philosophy.md) — vault keeps the navigable summary.

## The Three Pillars

Every Shishka dish, sourcing decision, and process must pass these three filters:

1. **Functional food** — every dish has a functional component (adaptogens, enzymes, superfoods). Food is medicine; meals are engineered for energy, gut health, nutrient density.
2. **Clean label** — sauces and dressings produced in-house from fermentation or cold-press. No industrial ingredients, no hidden oils, no artificial additives.
3. **Zero waste** — vegetable trim, stems, skins go into fermented bases, broths, probiotic shots. Both ethical and cost-optimization.

## Red Lines (never)

**Ingredients** — banned, no exceptions:
- Refined sugar
- Trans fats / refined seed oils (soy, canola/rapeseed, sunflower, corn) — **even when cheaper, even in dressings**
- Artificial colorants and preservatives
- MSG / monosodium glutamate

**Techniques** — forbidden:
- Deep frying
- Prolonged high-heat cooking that destroys micronutrients

**Styles** — refused:
- Heavy "comfort food" with carb overload
- Mayo-based sauces (replaced by aquafaba mayo, fermented dressings)

## Signature Principles (always)

- **CBS — Culinary Booster System** — every dish has a functional component, engineered as intent, not garnish. See [[Menu/Concept]].
- **Fibermaxxing** — every portion engineered for maximum fiber density. Fiber is a primary design constraint.
- **Texture contrast — the Crunch Element** — every dish must contain a deliberate crunch (seeds, fermented vegetables, activated nuts). Engineered, not accidental.
- **Clean Label** — all sauces in-house, fermentation or cold-press base. No store-bought industrial sauces. Ever.

## Quality Equation

```
Flavor + Functional + FC% = Success
```

Every dish must satisfy **all four**:

1. **Flavor balance** — all 5 tastes + umami
2. **Nutrient density** — high micronutrient-to-calorie ratio
3. **Plating aesthetic** — visual freshness ("Instagrammability 2026")
4. **Reproducibility** — line cook can assemble fast with no quality loss

Fail any → not menu-ready.

## Inspiration Sources

**Regional fusion** — three pillars:

- **Arab base** — hummus, labneh, spice work
- **Russian techniques** — fermentation, farm vegetables, grains
- **Thai local sourcing** — fruits, mushrooms (Lion's Mane), greens

**Movements:**
- *Food as Medicine*
- *Zero Waste* — vegetable trim → fermented bases, broth bases

## Cost vs Quality — Where Compromise Lives

> **Compromise is taken on logistics complexity, never on product quality.**

Concrete rule:

- Olive oil is **never** substituted with cheap rapeseed. Period.
- Optimization happens via **cross-utilization** (one ingredient feeding 5 dishes) and **local superfoods replacing imported ones** (local papaya/mango instead of imported berries).

When Chef Agent proposes a cost cut, it must come from cross-utilization or local sourcing — not from downgrading an ingredient class.

## Seasonality — the 80/20 Rule

Phuket seasonality is a function of **food cost** and **flavor**, not romanticism.

- **80% of menu** = stable Core (year-round, anchors LTV customers)
- **20% of menu** = seasonal rotations (Special Items) for novelty + local-calendar produce

Follow wet-season / dry-season calendar for fruits and greens.

## Fermentation & Preservation — Strategic Weapon

Critical role. Shishka's edge for gut health and food cost control.

L1 Kitchen (Zone 4: Cook-Chill Hub) produces in-house:
- Lacto-fermented vegetables
- Kombucha (planned, not immediate)
- Functional dressings

**Why it matters:**
- Reduces FC% (preserves surplus produce)
- Increases dish value without chemicals
- Differentiates from any competitor that buys sauces

## Plating vs Flavor — the 40/60 Ratio

> **Plating is the entry ticket. Flavor is what keeps them.**

- **40%** — visual (structure, color, freshness signal)
- **60%** — flavor and texture

A 9/10 visual with 6/10 flavor is a failure.

## Operational Protocols

### Protocol 1 — Sourcing Integrity
It's easy on Phuket to fall for ready-made sauces from Makro. **This will kill the brand.**

- No "hidden nasties" — only cold-pressed oils (Extra Virgin Olive, Avocado, Coconut). Never soy or rapeseed in dressings.
- Bases produced in-house at L1: aquafaba mayo, local-greens pesto, tahini-based dressings. **This is our barrier to entry against competitors.**

### Protocol 2 — Lego Modularity

For scaling (L1 → L2 → L-Infinity), the menu is built from **modules**:

| Module | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Bases** (Grains/Greens) | Standardized high-fiber foundations | Quinoa+kale base, sprouted lentil base |
| **Proteins** | Sous-vide in L1 (max yield, max juiciness, min FC) | Chicken, fish, plant proteins |
| **Toppings** (the Crunch) | Fermented vegetables, activated nuts and seeds | Sauerkraut, sprouted almonds |
| **Dressings** (the Soul) | Functional sauces with superfoods | Turmeric tahini, spirulina pesto |

Seasonal menu changes happen by swapping 1–2 modules — no staff retraining, no rewriting tech-cards for the entire base.

### Protocol 3 — L1 Technology Hub

Per Zone 4 Cook-Chill infrastructure:

- **Blast chilling** — every prep from the hot zone passes through. Result: 5–7 day shelf life with no preservatives, texture preserved.
- **Zero-waste fermentation** — vegetable trim (broccoli stems, cabbage cores, carrot skin) → fermentation tanks → probiotic shots and soup bases. Nothing leaves L1 as waste.
