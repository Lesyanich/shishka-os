## 1. Working time, rest & overtime (LPA B.E. 2541 + amendments)

**(A)** Normal limit **8 h/day and 48 h/week** for general (non-hazardous) work like a kitchen/café. A **rest break of at least 1 hour** must follow no more than **5 consecutive hours** of work (may be divided, must total ≥1 h). At least **one weekly holiday (day off)**, with no more than 6 days between weekly holidays. **Overtime requires the employee's consent** and is capped so that **OT + holiday work ≤ 36 h/week**.

Overtime/holiday premiums (on top of normal wage):
- OT on a normal working day → **×1.5** of hourly rate.
- Work on a weekly day off / public holiday → monthly-salaried staff get **+1× extra** (they are already paid for the day), daily-rated staff **+2×**.
- OT performed **on a holiday** → **×3**.

**(B) Shishka now:** Single-shift **08:00–17:00, 6 days (closed Mon)**, 60-min break = 8 h work × 6 = **48 h/week — exactly the legal max, no headroom.** Any extra hour is overtime. The `/hr/schedule` page flags `>48 h/week` and `<1 day off` automatically — keep it green.

**(C)** In a small kitchen the temptation is "just stay an extra hour." That hour is legally OT at ×1.5 and, unlogged, becomes a future back-pay claim. Either log/pay it or don't ask for it. Build coverage so nobody silently works 7 days — Thai staff often won't refuse a request to their face (kreng jai), so *you* must protect the day off, not wait for them to claim it.

## 2. Public holidays

**(A)** Private employers must grant **at least 13 paid public holidays per year** (LPA §29), and **National Labour Day (1 May) is mandatory** for the private sector. Holidays falling on a weekly day off get a **substitution (in-lieu) day**.

**Official Thailand public holidays 2026 (B.E. 2569):**

|---|---|
| 1 Jan | New Year's Day |
| 3 Mar | Makha Bucha ☸ |
| 6 Apr | Chakri Memorial Day |
| 13–15 Apr | **Songkran** (Thai New Year) |
| 1 May | **National Labour Day** (mandatory private sector) |
| 4 May | Coronation Day |

☸ = Buddhist lunar dates may shift by ±1 day on final confirmation. Alcohol sales are **banned on the major Buddhist holy days** (Makha/Visakha/Asalha Bucha + Lent) — relevant if Shishka ever sells alcohol.

**(B) Shishka choice:** From this list **designate ≥13 as paid company holidays** (must include 1 May). Because we are closed Mondays anyway, a holiday landing on a Monday is "free." Publish the chosen 13+ at the start of the year so staff can plan.

**(C)** **Songkran (13–15 Apr) is non-negotiable in practice** — it is the biggest family holiday; most Thai staff travel home and will effectively take ~a week. Plan for reduced staffing or closure around it months ahead, not the week before. Don't fight it; budget for it. Birthdays of the King/Queen and Buddhist holy days carry real cultural weight — treat them with respect, not as "lost revenue."

## 3. Wages & payslips

**(A)** **Phuket minimum wage = ฿400/day** (highest tier; unchanged for 2026). Wages must be paid **at least monthly** unless agreed otherwise, on time, in Thai baht. For monthly-salaried staff the hourly rate for OT = monthly salary ÷ (days × 8). Payslips should itemise gross, OT, deductions (SSO, advances), and net.

**(B)** Shishka payroll runs through `fn_calculate_payroll` ([hr-payroll-spec.md](../plans/hr-payroll-spec.md)): monthly salary pro-rated by unpaid absences, OT ×1.5, SSO deduction, advances netted. Min-wage check: ฿400/day × ~26 days ≈ **฿10,400/month floor** for full-time — every Shishka salary (Mint ฿25k, cooks ฿15k, helper ฿12k) clears it.

**(C)** Cash culture: Thai line staff often expect **salary advances** mid-month and prefer cash. Track every advance (we have `salary_advances`) or it becomes "I never got that." Pay **on the promised day, every time** — late pay destroys trust faster than a low wage, and a payslip in hand prevents disputes.

## 4. Social Security (SSO) & tax

**(A)** **Social Security Act B.E. 2533.** Employee 5% + employer 5%. **2026 wage ceiling = ฿17,500** (raised from ฿15,000), so **max contribution = ฿875/month each** (phase 1, 2026–2028; later ฿20,000→฿1,000, then ฿23,000→฿1,150). The employer must **register a new employee with the SSO within 30 days of hiring** and remit both shares monthly (by the 15th of the following month). Covers healthcare, maternity, disability, unemployment, old-age pension.

Personal income tax (PND): employer withholds per Revenue Department tables for staff above the tax-free threshold and files PND.1; most line staff at ฿12–25k/month fall at/near zero withholding. (Tax detail lives in finance docs — this is a pointer.)

**(B) Onboarding step (mandatory):** register each new hire with SSO within 30 days; set `staff.sso_enrolled = true` and store `sso_number`. Until enrolled, SSO deductions should not run (the payroll RPC gates on `ssf_enrolled`).

**(C)** Foreign owners sometimes skip SSO "to save 5%." Don't — non-registration is a compliance violation, and the 4:1 work-permit framework *requires* the company to be SSO-registered anyway (§7). SSO is also a genuine staff benefit (free hospital, maternity) — used as a selling point in hiring.

## 5. Hiring foreigners — the constraint that shapes Shishka ⚠

**(A)** To employ a foreigner on a work permit, a Thai limited company generally needs **4 Thai employees + ฿2,000,000 registered capital per work permit**, and must be registered for SSO and VAT. (Reduced 50% — 2 Thai + ฿1M — if the foreigner is married to a Thai national. **BOI-promoted** companies are exempt from the quota/capital minimums.)

**Reserved occupations:** a list of jobs is **reserved for Thai nationals** — including **manual labour, retail/sales trading, and cashier/clerical-type front-of-house work**. A foreigner's permit also ties them to the **specific role and employer** stated on it.

**(B) What this means for Shishka right now:**
- **Cashier / handling money / sales = Thai nationals only.** Mint is currently the *only* employee who can legally take payment. Lesia (foreign GM) and the Myanmar cooks **cannot** operate the till — not a preference, a legal line.
- Myanmar staff (Hein/Alex/Nono) work under the **migrant-worker (MOU/Section 64-type) regime**, which is administered differently from skilled-expat permits but still **binds them to kitchen/permitted duties** — they must not be put on cash/sales.
- The **4:1 ratio** means our foreign headcount (Lesia + 3 Myanmar) implies a **large required Thai headcount** to be fully compliant. This is the single biggest open compliance question. → **`/lawyer` to confirm exact ratio math, MOU specifics, and our exposure.**

**(C)** This is why "hire a 2nd Thai" is rated *critical*, not nice-to-have: with one Thai cashier the business's ability to take money rests on one person on probation. Recruit Thai staff through **personal networks/referrals** (how hiring actually works here) and keep the till legally covered at all times.

## 6. Probation, discipline, termination & severance ⚠

**(A) Probation:** Thai law has **no separate "probation" status** — a probationer is a normal employee. The practical hinge is the **120-day** mark: dismiss **before 120 days of service** → **no statutory severance** (but still give the contractual/statutory **notice** or pay in lieu). After 120 days, the severance scale applies.

**Severance (dismissal without cause), by continuous service:**

| Service length | Severance |
|---|---|
| 120 days – <1 year | **30 days'** wages |
| 1 – <3 years | **90 days'** |
| 3 – <6 years | **180 days'** |
| 6 – <10 years | **240 days'** |
| 10 – <20 years | **300 days'** |
| **≥20 years** | **400 days'** |

**Notice:** at least **one pay period** in advance, or pay in lieu. For dismissal on **restructuring/technology grounds**, **60 days'** advance notice (or 60 days' pay) plus possible special severance.

**Dismissal for cause (no severance, §119):** dishonesty/intentional crime against the employer; intentional damage; gross negligence causing serious loss; violating lawful written work rules **after a written warning** (or a serious violation, no warning needed); **3 consecutive working days' absence without reasonable cause**; imprisonment by final judgment. Cause must be **stated in writing at the time of dismissal** or it generally can't be relied on later.

Even a lawful dismissal can be challenged as **"unfair termination"** at the Labour Court, which can order damages or reinstatement.

**(B) Termination playbook (do it in this order):**
1. **Document first.** Performance/conduct issues → written warning(s), signed/acknowledged, kept on file. No paper trail = no "cause."
2. Decide: *for cause* (§119, documented) vs *without cause* (pay severance + notice).
3. Calculate **notice (or pay in lieu) + severance** per the table; final pay (wages + severance) is due **promptly on dismissal** (within days, not next payroll).
4. **De-register from SSO**; set `fire_date`, `is_active=false`.
5. **Deliver it privately, calmly, face-saving** (see C). Hand over a written statement of the reason.

**(C)** Firing in Thailand is as much cultural as legal. **Never dismiss or dress someone down in front of others** — public humiliation (loss of face) can turn a routine exit into a Labour Court complaint and poison the rest of the team. Do it one-on-one, quietly, let the person keep dignity, and be generous/correct on the final payment — word travels in the local Thai labour network and shapes whether you can hire again. When in doubt on amounts or "cause", **pay the severance** rather than gamble on a Labour Court loss.

## 7. Rights & obligations (both sides)

| Employer must | Employee must |
|---|---|
| Pay agreed wages **on time**, ≥ minimum wage | Perform agreed duties with reasonable care |
| Give weekly day off, holidays, statutory leave | Follow lawful work rules & safety instructions |
| Provide a safe workplace | Not commit dishonesty/intentional harm |
| Register & remit **SSO** (within 30 days) | Give notice when resigning (≥1 pay period) |
| Keep **written work rules in Thai** if ≥10 staff; file the **annual employment report each January** (LPA No.9, ≥10 staff) | Not be absent ≥3 consecutive days without cause |
| Pay **notice + severance** on no-cause dismissal | Respect confidentiality / company property |

## 8. Thai work culture — managing Thai staff (the manager brain)

The law sets the floor; **culture decides whether your team stays and performs.** Core concepts:

- **Kreng jai (เกรงใจ)** — deep reluctance to impose, contradict, or burden a superior. Staff will **say "yes" / "no problem" to your face even when it's a problem**, won't report issues upward, and won't refuse extra work openly. *Information flows down easily; it struggles to flow up.* You must actively pull problems out, not wait to be told.
- **Face / sia naa (เสียหน้า)** — protecting dignity in front of others is paramount. **Never criticise, correct sharply, or fire someone publicly.** Praise in public, correct in private, always.
- **Hierarchy (phu yai / phu noi)** — seniority and the "big person/small person" relationship are real; the boss is expected to be both authoritative *and* caring (a benevolent elder), not a peer.
- **Bunkhun (บุญคุณ)** — reciprocal gratitude/loyalty. Genuine care (helping with a family emergency, a fair advance) earns deep loyalty that money alone won't buy.
- **Sanuk (สนุก)** — work should have an element of enjoyment/lightness. A grim, tense kitchen loses people; a friendly one retains them.
- **Mai pen rai (ไม่เป็นไร)** — "no worries / never mind." Smooths conflict but can also mask unresolved problems.

**How to manage with this, not against it:**
- Give feedback **indirectly and privately**; frame as "let's improve together," never as blame in front of the team.
- Create safe, **specific** ways for staff to flag problems (one-on-one check-ins, a quiet word) — they won't volunteer bad news in a group.
- Be the **reliable, caring boss**: pay on time, remember names/families, handle the advance fairly. That, plus respect, beats a slightly higher wage elsewhere.
- **Younger Thai workers** increasingly value psychological safety and purpose and view *excessive* kreng jai as inefficiency — they'll quietly leave a place that feels stifling. Balance respect for hierarchy with room to actually do good work.

## 9. Pitfalls & heuristics (experienced-GM shortlist)

1. **"Yes" ≠ agreement, "mai pen rai" ≠ resolved.** Confirm understanding by having them explain it back, not by asking "ok?"
2. **Protect the weekly day off yourself** — staff won't claim it; unclaimed ≠ waived in law.
3. **Never criticise/fire in public.** One face-losing scene can cost you the whole team's morale and a Labour Court case.
4. **Pay day is sacred.** Late or wrong pay breaks trust irreversibly. Always issue a payslip.
5. **Log every advance and every OT hour** — undocumented = future back-pay claim.
6. **Document before you discipline.** No written warning, no "cause."
7. **When unsure on severance, pay it.** Cheaper than losing at Labour Court.
8. **Plan Songkran (and major holidays) months ahead** — expect ~a week of thin staffing.
9. **Hire Thais through referrals/network** — the most reliable channel here; treat departing staff well so the network stays open.
10. **Keep the till legally Thai-staffed at all times** — never let a foreigner cover cash, even "just for an hour."
11. **Register SSO within 30 days** of every hire — it's compliance *and* a hiring perk.
12. **Ghosting happens** — a staff member may simply stop showing up rather than resign to your face; reduce it with a friendly culture and by surfacing problems early.
